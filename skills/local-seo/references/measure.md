# Baseline and proof

Local SEO work fails in week three, not because it did not work, but because nobody wrote down where they started. Fix that before changing anything.

---

## Proximity, which is the thing nobody tells them

There is no such thing as "ranking number one" in the map. Map results are heavily weighted by where the searcher is standing. A business can be first from two blocks away and invisible from across the river, in the same minute.

Say this out loud, at the start, in plain words:

> Your ranking is different for every person depending on where they are when they search. Someone near your shop may see you first while someone across town does not see you at all. That is normal and it is not something to fix. What you can move is how wide that circle is.

This matters for two reasons. It stops them checking their own ranking from their own shop and thinking everything is fine. And it stops anyone selling them a "number one rankings guaranteed" package, which is always either a lie or a search nobody types.

Never report a ranking position as a fact without saying where it was searched from.

---

## The baseline, taken before anything changes

Five things, written down with today's date. It takes ten minutes and it is the only way anyone will know later whether this worked.

1. **Review count, average, and the date of the newest one.** Screenshot the panel.
2. **Photo count, and the date of the newest photo.**
3. **The keyword list.** Three to five searches a real customer would actually type. Not "best plumber", which nobody types. `emergency plumber edmonton`, `burst pipe repair south edmonton`.
4. **Where they appear for each**, and from where the search was run. "Not in the map pack, ninth in the regular results, searched from downtown."
5. **A screenshot of the Google Business Profile performance tab.** Calls, direction requests, website clicks, and the searches people used to find them. This is the only first-party data in the whole exercise and it is free.

Put all five in the report as a dated "where you are today" block. It is the before photo.

---

## Tag the profile link so the profile can be proved

The website link on the Google Business Profile can carry tracking on the end of it. Almost nobody does this, and it is the difference between "I think the profile is working" and knowing.

```
https://theirsite.com/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp
```

Paste that as the website URL on the profile. From then on, every visit that came from the profile is separated out in their analytics. Add a second one for the appointment or menu link if they use it.

Say what it does in one line: it adds a label to the link so you can tell which visitors came from your Google listing rather than guessing.

---

## What to expect, and when

Be honest about this at the start. Half the reason people quit is that they were sold a faster timeline than exists.

| Change | When it moves |
|---|---|
| Primary category | Days. Sometimes hours. |
| Hours, services, attributes, photos | Days |
| New reviews arriving steadily | Two to six weeks, and it compounds |
| Posts | Immediate visibility on the panel, small ranking effect |
| New service and area pages on the site | Four weeks to three months |
| Citation cleanup | Slow, and it is a floor-raiser rather than a jump |

The honest summary: the free profile work can move within a week, and the website work is a quarter. Anyone promising rankings by Friday is lying.

---

## The check-in

Set one date, thirty days out, and one job for it: take the same five measurements again and put them next to the first set.

Not a dashboard. Not weekly. One repeat measurement, compared to the baseline, with the same searches run from the same place. That is enough to tell whether to keep going or change approach, and it is little enough that it actually gets done.
