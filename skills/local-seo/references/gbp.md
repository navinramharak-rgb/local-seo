# The Google Business Profile

The highest leverage hour in local marketing, and the one almost nobody finishes.

## What you can and cannot see

You cannot open someone's dashboard. You can see the public listing. Work from the public listing and say that is what you did.

To see it:
- Search `"[business name] [city]"` and read the panel that comes back
- Search `"[main service] [city]"` and see who fills the three map results
- Open the competitor they named and compare, field by field

If no listing comes back at all, stop everything else. Claiming or creating it is the entire first job, and it is free.

## Category, first and most important

The **primary category** is the strongest single ranking factor in the map results. Everything else is downstream.

Rules:
- Primary category = the single thing that makes the most money, in Google's words, not the owner's. Google has a fixed list. "Bookkeeper" and "Accountant" are different categories with different competition.
- Pick the most specific one that is still accurate. Too broad loses to specialists, too narrow misses the main search.
- Then add secondary categories for the other real services. They help, and they cost nothing.
- Do not add categories for things the business does not do. It can get the listing suspended.

The usual finding: the primary category is generic or simply wrong, and the business has been losing its main search term for years without knowing. Check this before anything else.

## Storefront or service area

Get this right or the listing gets suppressed.

- **Storefront:** customers come to you. Show the address. Set hours.
- **Service area:** you go to them. **Hide the address.** Set the service areas. A service-area business showing a home address is a common cause of suppression.
- **Both** (a shop that also travels): show the address and set service areas.

If they answered question 3 with "we go to them" and the listing shows a street address, that is a top-three finding.

## Field by field

**Name.** The real business name, exactly as on the sign. Adding keywords ("Star Wash Edmonton Best Car Wash") is against policy and is the most commonly reported and penalised violation. If a competitor is doing it, that is reportable, and worth mentioning as an option.

**Address.** Exact, and matching the website and every directory character for character. "Suite 4" vs "#4" vs "Unit 4" counts as three different businesses to some systems.

**Phone.** A local number beats a toll-free one. Same number everywhere. A call-tracking number that differs from the website number breaks consistency.

**Hours.** Real ones. Add holiday hours, because Google surfaces "hours might differ" warnings that push people to a competitor who filled it in.

**Description.** 750 characters. What you do, where, who for, what makes you different. Not a keyword list. Most of it is not shown, so front-load it.

**Services.** List every one, each with its own short description. This is a big and mostly empty field on most listings. Fill it.

**Products.** Works for service businesses too, with prices or ranges. Takes up real estate in the panel that competitors leave blank.

**Photos.** The most-neglected ranking and conversion signal. Real ones, not stock. Exterior so people can find the door, interior, team, and the work itself. Recent matters: listings with photos from this month look alive. Aim for a few new ones monthly.

**Attributes.** Wheelchair accessible, women-owned, free parking, appointment required, languages spoken. Free, filterable, and they show as badges.

**Q&A.** Anyone can ask, and anyone can answer, which means a competitor or a crank can define the business. Seed it: post the ten questions customers actually ask and answer them from the business account.

**Posts.** Expire after seven days. Weekly is enough. Offers, jobs completed, seasonal notes. Evidence of an active business.

**Messaging.** Turn it on only if someone will actually reply. A slow reply rate is worse than no messaging.

## What to report

For each field: what the listing currently shows, what it should show, and how long the fix takes. Group into "do today, free, one hour" versus everything else.

Then the one line that matters: **the single change most likely to move the map ranking.** Nearly always the primary category.

## The fields almost nobody fills in

Category is the biggest lever. These are the next ones, and they are all free, all fast, and mostly empty on the average listing. A profile with these filled beats a better-known competitor with a bare one more often than people expect.

**Services.** Each service is its own entry with its own short description. Most listings have none. Every entry is a term Google can match against a search, and it is the cheapest reach available. List the money services first.

**Products, with prices.** The products section takes up real estate on the panel and almost nobody uses it. A price, even a starting price, filters out the wrong enquiries and gives the right ones a reason to call.

**The description.** 750 characters. Say what they do, where, and who for, in the first sentence, because that is all that shows before "more". Not a mission statement.

**Attributes.** Wheelchair accessible, free parking, appointment required, women-owned, veteran-owned, LGBTQ+ friendly, on-site services. Some of these are their own filter in Google Maps, which means a business without them is not in that filtered list at all.

**Q&A.** Anyone can ask a question on a listing, and anyone can answer. Unanswered questions sit there in public, sometimes for years, sometimes answered wrong by a stranger. The owner is allowed to post their own questions and answer them, and that is the right way to handle the five things customers always ask: parking, price range, whether they take walk-ins, what areas they cover, how fast they respond.

**Booking and messaging links.** If they take bookings, the link goes on the profile. If they can answer a text within a business day, turn messaging on. If they cannot, leave it off, because a slow reply on a visible channel is worse than no channel.

**Posts.** Free, visible on the panel, ignored by nearly everyone. A post every week or two, showing a real job with a real photo, keeps the listing looking alive. The ranking effect is small. The looks-open-and-active effect is not.

**Hours, including holidays.** Wrong hours is the fastest way to a one-star review. Special hours for holidays are a separate field and it is nearly always blank.

**Photos, and their recency.** Both matter. A listing whose newest photo is three years old reads as closed. Real photos of real work beat stock every time, and geotagging them does nothing despite what a lot of advice says.

## Verify with a second pair of eyes

Before reporting a listing field as missing, check the listing a second way: the search panel, Google Maps directly, and the business's own share link if they have one. Panels render differently across surfaces and something can look absent when it is simply not shown in that view.
