# Reviews

The strongest local lever that is also a sales asset. Ranking, and the thing a customer reads before calling.

## What to assess

- How many, versus the competitor they named
- **How recent.** The most-missed metric. 40 reviews with the newest from 2024 reads worse than 12 from the last quarter.
- Average, and whether it is believable. A flat 5.0 across 200 reviews reads fake to customers.
- Does the owner reply? To all, or only the angry ones?
- What do the bad ones actually say? Recurring themes are operational feedback, not just a ranking problem.

## The ask

The moment matters more than the wording. Ask when the customer is happiest: the job just finished and they are pleased, not when the invoice lands.

Get the short link from the profile ("Ask for reviews" gives a `g.page/r/...` link). A link that takes one tap converts several times better than "find us on Google".

Text beats email for trades and services. Keep it to two sentences:

```
Hey [name], thanks again for today. If you've got 30 seconds,
a quick Google review really helps people find us: [link]
```

For a clinic or a firm, slightly warmer, same length. Send it the same day.

## Volume

Steady beats a burst. Ten reviews in one afternoon after two silent years looks bought, and Google's filters agree. A handful a week, forever, is the target.

Make it somebody's actual job with an actual trigger, or it stops in week two.

## Replying

Reply to all of them. It is a ranking signal and every future customer reads the replies.

Good review: short, specific, use their name, mention the actual job. Never copy and paste the same line.

```
Thanks Dave. That back deck was a big one and the weather did not
help. Glad it came together. Enjoy the summer out there.
```

Bad review: this one is read by everyone who ever considers hiring them. Do not win the argument.

```
I'm sorry, that's not the experience we want anyone to have. I'd like
to sort it out. I'm at [number] and I'll answer myself. [Name], owner.
```

Never argue, never blame the customer, never share private details of their file. One reply, then take it offline. A calm reply under a harsh review sells better than the five-star ones above it.

## Never do these

- Offer a discount, entry, freebie or anything else in exchange for a review. Against policy, and reportable.
- Write reviews for the business, or ask staff and family to.
- Buy them.
- **Review gating:** asking how it went first and only sending the happy ones to Google. This includes the "feedback funnel" software that sells itself for exactly this. Against policy.
- Bulk-request an old customer list in one go.

If the user asks for any of these, say plainly that it risks the listing, then give them the version that works. Do not build the gating flow.
