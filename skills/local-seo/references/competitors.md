# The competitor teardown

This is the part that turns local SEO advice into something a business owner can act on. Without it you are handing over a list of best practices. With it you are handing over "here is exactly what the three businesses beating you are doing that you are not."

Do this before you write a single recommendation.

---

## Step 1. Find the real competitors, not the ones they named

The competitor they named in question 5 is the one that annoys them. It is often not the one actually taking the searches.

Search the money keyword the way a customer would type it: `[main service] [city]`. Then `[main service] near me` if you can, and one or two variations they would realistically use. Look at the map pack, which is the block of three businesses with the map above the normal results.

Take the three in the map pack. Add the one they named if it is not already in there. That is your comparison set.

**Say which searches you ran.** The whole teardown is only as good as the query, and the owner needs to be able to repeat it.

---

## Step 2. Pull the same fields for everyone, including them

Build one table. Every row is a field, every column is a business, theirs first. Anything you could not see gets "couldn't check", never a guess.

| Field | Why it decides who wins |
|---|---|
| Primary category | The single strongest ranking factor in the map |
| Secondary categories | Free reach into adjacent searches |
| Review count | Volume signal and the first thing a customer compares |
| Average rating | Below 4.0 costs calls, a flat 5.0 across hundreds reads fake |
| Newest review | Recency is the half of this nobody measures |
| Owner replies | Visible effort, and Google notices the activity |
| Photo count | Listings with more photos get more clicks and more direction requests |
| Newest photo | A profile whose last photo is three years old looks closed |
| Hours filled in | An empty or wrong hours field suppresses the listing |
| Services listed | Each one is a searchable term most people leave blank |
| Products with prices | Almost nobody fills this in, and it takes up real space on the panel |
| Posts, and how recent | Free, ignored by nearly everyone, visible on the panel |
| Q&A, and whether answered | Unanswered questions sit there in public |
| Booking or messaging link | Removes a step between seeing you and contacting you |
| Attributes | Wheelchair access, free parking, women-owned, appointment required |
| Website, and does it have a page for this service | Where the listing sends the click |
| Description | Whether it says what they do and where, or is boilerplate |

---

## Step 3. Turn it into a gap list, not a table dump

A table is research. The deliverable is the three or four gaps that are costing them the search, ranked.

For each gap, say it in this shape:

> **Category.** You are listed as "Car Detailing Service". All three businesses above you in the map are listed as "Car Wash". That is the search you want and the category you are not in. Change the primary category, keep detailing as a secondary. Free, takes two minutes, and it is the single biggest thing on this page.

> **Review recency.** You have 41 reviews, more than two of them. Your newest is from March. Theirs are from this month. Volume is not your problem, freshness is.

Gaps that are actually just "they are bigger than you" are not useful. Skip them. Focus on the ones that are a decision, not a budget.

---

## Step 4. Say what you could not see

You cannot open anyone's dashboard, including your own client's. You are reading public listings. Some things are genuinely invisible from outside:

- How complete their services and products lists are, beyond what the panel displays
- Whether they run ads, and how much of the map position is paid
- Their insights, their search terms, their conversion data
- Whether reviews were earned or bought

Say so plainly in the report. A teardown that admits its own limits is more useful than one that pretends to omniscience, and the owner will trust the rest of it more.

---

## The one thing to avoid

Do not tell them to copy a competitor's category if the category is not accurate for their business. A wrong category can get a listing suspended, which is a far worse outcome than ranking fourth. Accuracy first, then specificity.
