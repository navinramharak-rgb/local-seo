# The plan document

The output of this skill is not a conversation. It is one file the owner can open on a Tuesday, work through, and hand to whoever does the website.

**Start from `report-template.html`.** Copy it, fill every `{{TOKEN}}`, delete the blocks you have nothing for. Do not design a new layout. Search the finished file for `{{` before handing it over.

---

## Hard rules for the file

- **One self-contained HTML file.** No external stylesheets, scripts, fonts or images.
- **Print works.** People print these and work through them with a pen.
- **Name it** `[business-name]-local-search-plan-[YYYY-MM-DD].html`.
- **Every number traceable.** Review counts, photo counts, ranking positions and competitor figures all came from something you actually looked at. If you could not see it, the cell says "couldn't check", not a guess.

---

## Order

1. **Masthead** — business, current primary category, city, score ring.
2. **Do this first** — the single highest-leverage action, on its own, above everything. Nearly always the primary category.
3. **Where you are today** — the five baseline measurements, dated, with the proximity caveat.
4. **The competition** — the side-by-side table, then three or four gap cards.
5. **The scorecard** — six areas, scored, with one honest finding each.
6. **What I couldn't check.**
7. **The ninety day plan** — today, this week, this month.
8. **How you'll know it worked** — the repeat measurement and the tagged profile link.

The reason "do this first" sits above the research: an owner reads the top and skims the rest. If the category is wrong, that one change is worth more than the other forty lines combined, and it needs to be impossible to miss.

---

## The scorecard

Six areas, weighted by what actually moves the map.

| Area | Weight | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Profile completeness | 30 | Every field filled: hours, services, products, description, attributes, photos, Q&A, messaging |
| Category fit | 20 | Is the primary category the one the money search actually uses |
| Reviews | 20 | Count, average, recency, and whether the owner replies |
| Local pages | 15 | A real page per money service and per area worth having |
| NAP and citations | 10 | Name, address and phone identical everywhere, old listings cleaned up |
| Schema and measurement | 5 | LocalBusiness markup, and a tagged profile link so any of this can be proved |

Set `{{SCORE_DASH}}` to `SCORE × 3.39` then a space then `339`. A 48 is `163 339`.

Bar colour: no class above 70%, `mid` between 40 and 70, `low` below 40.

Score it honestly. Most businesses that ask for this land in the 30s and 40s, and that is the point: it is a before photo, and a generous score makes the after photo meaningless.

---

## How a gap card is written

Three parts.

**What they do.** Concrete and quoted from the listing. "All three businesses above you are listed under the primary category Car Wash."

**Why it costs.** In searches and calls, not in rules. "That is the search 'car wash edmonton', which is the one your customers type. You are not in that category, so you are not in that result."

**The fix.** One action, specific, with the effort attached. "Change your primary category to Car Wash and keep Car Detailing Service as a secondary. Two minutes, free, and it is the biggest single thing on this page."

Three or four gap cards. Not ten. A gap that is really just "they have been here longer" is not actionable, so cut it.

---

## Tone

Write to the owner. Second person. Short sentences.

No "leverage", "unlock", "game-changer" or "supercharge". No em dashes.

Translate every term on first use in the same sentence: "your primary category, which is the one label Google uses to decide which searches you show up in".

Set the timeline honestly, in the document, not just in conversation. Profile changes move in days, website changes take a quarter. Say it where they will read it in week three when they are wondering whether to give up.

---

## What not to put in it

- No invented rankings, search volumes, review counts or competitor figures.
- No ranking claimed without saying where it was searched from.
- No review gating, incentives, or anything else that risks the listing, even if the user asks for it.
- No "do all 300 directories".
- No placeholder left in the file.
