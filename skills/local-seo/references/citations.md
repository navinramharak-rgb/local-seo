# Citations

A citation is the business listed somewhere with its name, address and phone. Consistency matters more than quantity.

## Find the wrong ones first

Old listings actively work against them. Before adding anything:

- Search the business name in quotes
- Search the phone number
- Search any **previous** address or phone

Anything with an old address or a dead number gets fixed or removed. This is worth more than twenty new listings.

## The ones that matter

Core:
- Google Business Profile
- Bing Places
- Apple Business Connect (this is what Apple Maps and Siri use, and most businesses have never heard of it)
- Facebook Page
- Instagram bio

Canada specifically:
- Yellow Pages Canada
- Canada411
- 411.ca
- The local chamber of commerce
- The city's own business directory, if there is one

Most American local SEO advice skips the Canadian ones entirely. For an Edmonton business they are worth more than half the US directories.

Then the trade-specific ones, which usually outperform the general directories:
- Trades: HomeStars, TrustedPros, the relevant licensing body
- Legal: the provincial law society directory, Lawyer Referral
- Medical and dental: the provincial college, RateMDs
- Restaurants: OpenTable, Yelp, the delivery platforms
- Auto: CarGurus and the manufacturer locators

## Rules

- Same NAP format everywhere, character for character
- Same primary category where the directory has one
- Same description, or close
- Link to the website
- Real photos where they are allowed

## What not to do

Do not buy a 300-directory submission package. Most of those sites have no traffic and no authority, several actively republish bad data, and cleaning it up later takes longer than it took to create.

Twelve correct listings beat three hundred sloppy ones.
